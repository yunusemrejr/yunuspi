# Growth, SEO and distribution

Great products still fail without distribution. Growth is the systematic discovery of repeatable ways to acquire, activate and retain customers—channels that fit the market, loops that compound, and experiments that separate real wins from noise.

## Find channel-market fit {#channel-fit}
<!-- terms: channel market fit distribution acquisition channel cac ltv focus test bullseye -->

**Principle.** Test a few channels cheaply, measure acquisition cost and quality, and concentrate on the one or two that fit how the target customers already discover products.

**Why.** Channels behave like power laws: one usually outperforms all others combined. Where the audience already looks for solutions—search, communities, marketplaces, partners, outbound—determines fit. Spreading effort across every channel yields mediocre results everywhere. Unit economics (customer acquisition cost versus lifetime value, payback period) decide whether a channel scales.

**Signals.** Effort spread across many channels; channels chosen by habit; no cost-per-acquisition measurement.

**Ask.** Where do these customers discover solutions today, and which channel shows the best cost and quality so far?

**Traps.** Abandoning channels before tests were large enough to learn from.

## Technical SEO is table stakes {#technical-seo}
<!-- terms: technical seo crawl index sitemap robots canonical meta title description structured data schema speed core web vitals ssr -->

**Principle.** Make pages crawlable and indexable: server-rendered or static HTML, unique titles and descriptions, canonical URLs, sitemaps, structured data, fast loading and clean internal links.

**Why.** Content that search engines cannot render or understand cannot rank. Client-rendered pages, duplicate URLs without canonicals, missing titles and slow pages quietly suppress traffic. Structured data enables rich results; internal linking distributes authority and helps discovery. These are one-time engineering investments with compounding returns.

**Signals.** Marketing content rendered client-side only; duplicate titles; no sitemap; important pages orphaned from internal links.

**Ask.** Can a crawler see this page's content in the HTML, and does it have a unique title, description and canonical URL?

**Traps.** Keyword stuffing and other tactics that search engines penalize.

## Write for search intent, not keywords {#search-intent}
<!-- terms: search intent informational navigational commercial transactional keyword research long tail topic cluster serp -->

**Principle.** For each target query, determine what the searcher actually wants (to learn, compare, buy or navigate) and create the best page for that intent.

**Why.** Search engines rank pages that satisfy searchers. A product page cannot rank for "how to" queries; a tutorial will not rank for "best X for Y" comparisons. Looking at what already ranks reveals the expected format. Long-tail queries with specific intent convert better and are easier to win. Topic clusters—pillar pages with linked subtopic pages—build authority.

**Signals.** Content written around keywords without checking what ranks; product pages targeting informational queries.

**Ask.** What does someone searching this query want, and is this page the best answer to that intent?

**Traps.** Chasing high-volume head terms dominated by giants.

## Build content that compounds {#compounding-content}
<!-- terms: content marketing evergreen compound library templates tools calculators guides resource programmatic -->

**Principle.** Invest in evergreen assets that keep attracting the right audience—definitive guides, templates, free tools, calculators, benchmarks—over ephemeral posts.

**Why.** A news post decays in days; a useful tool or definitive guide attracts links and traffic for years. Free tools that solve a small adjacent problem ("invoice generator", "regex tester") capture intent near the product and demonstrate capability. Programmatic pages can scale this for data-rich products, but only when each page is genuinely useful.

**Signals.** Content calendars full of short-lived posts; no content that solves a problem on its own.

**Ask.** Will this content still attract the right people in two years?

**Traps.** Thin programmatic pages that search engines treat as spam.

## Engineer product-led loops {#loops}
<!-- terms: product led growth plg viral loop invite collaboration sharing referral network effect watermark free tier -->

**Principle.** Design the product so using it naturally exposes it to new users: collaboration invites, shared artifacts, public outputs, integrations and referral incentives.

**Why.** Loops compound where funnels do not: each user brings more users. Collaboration tools grow through invites; design tools through shared files; developer tools through open-source usage and badges. A free tier or trial lowers the barrier to entering the loop. Measuring the loop's cycle time and conversion at each step shows where to invest.

**Signals.** No sharing or collaboration features in a product whose output others see; referral programs bolted on without product integration.

**Ask.** How does one user's successful use of the product bring in the next user?

**Traps.** Forced sharing that annoys users; incentives that attract low-quality sign-ups.

## Community is a moat when it serves members {#community}
<!-- terms: community discord slack forum reddit hacker news open source contributors ambassadors events engagement -->

**Principle.** Participate in communities by being useful first—answering questions, sharing knowledge, building in public—and build your own community around members' goals, not your announcements.

**Why.** Communities distrust promotion and reward genuine contribution. Reputation built by helping converts into attention when launching. Owned communities retain customers and generate feedback, content and advocacy, but only when members get value from each other. Each platform has norms (Hacker News prizes technical depth, Reddit punishes self-promotion) that must be respected.

**Signals.** Community posts that are only announcements; ignoring platform rules on self-promotion; a community channel with no member interaction.

**Ask.** What value does this community participation provide to members, independent of promoting the product?

**Traps.** Astroturfing with fake accounts or paid posts disguised as organic.

## Lifecycle messaging drives activation and retention {#lifecycle}
<!-- terms: email lifecycle onboarding drip nurture activation retention churn winback segmentation trigger behavioral -->

**Principle.** Send messages triggered by user behavior—onboarding steps, milestones, inactivity—segmented by what users did, instead of generic newsletters.

**Why.** Behavioral messages arrive when they are relevant: a nudge when setup stalls, a tip after a first success, a win-back after inactivity. They outperform time-based blasts dramatically. Retention compounds growth more than acquisition: a small reduction in churn raises lifetime value across all customers.

**Signals.** Only newsletters; no onboarding emails; churned users never contacted; the same message to every user.

**Ask.** Which user behaviors should trigger a helpful message, and what should each message help them do next?

**Traps.** Over-messaging that trains users to ignore or unsubscribe.

## Instrument and experiment with discipline {#analytics}
<!-- terms: analytics events tracking funnel cohort attribution experiment ab test conversion rate optimization cro -->

**Principle.** Track events tied to decisions, analyze by cohorts and funnels, and run properly powered experiments on changes that matter.

**Why.** Vanity metrics obscure what drives growth; funnels show where users drop; cohorts show whether changes improve retention over time. Growth teams often run many small tests without enough traffic, producing noise mistaken for wins. Prioritize high-impact experiments, pre-register metrics, and respect privacy and consent in tracking.

**Signals.** Tracking everything but analyzing nothing; experiments declared winners on small samples; attribution debates without data.

**Ask.** What decision will this data or experiment inform, and is it large enough to detect a real effect?

**Traps.** Optimizing conversion at the expense of customer quality.
